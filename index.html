Why it exists

Expert calls are won and lost in a few seconds.

You know the subject. That is why you were screened in. The hard part is the moment a question lands from an odd angle and the client is waiting.

01

The half-remembered number

The client asks for a figure you know roughly. Guess, and you risk contradicting what you wrote in your screening answers.

02

The question from an odd angle

A topic you know well, asked in a way you did not expect. The silence stretches while you find your way in.

03

Sounding like the wrong expert

Answer like a market analyst when they screened in an operator or a buyer, and the call can end early.

What makes it different

An answer assistant built for live calls.

Not a note-taker and not a chatbot. One answer at a time, in your voice, with its source in plain sight.

Every answer says what it rests on.

You can see at a glance how far to trust a card before you say a word of it.

  • Where an answer draws on your own material, a green label names the source and your exact words are quoted underneath.
  • Everything the AI writes is marked not verified, with a dashed amber rule, so you know what to check.
  • Nothing reaches your screen without one of these labels. If the app cannot say what an answer rests on, it shows nothing.

Written to be read aloud

Short sentences, one to a line, the direct answer first. Checked for length before it reaches your screen.

A line to start with

While the full answer is written and checked, a line you can begin with appears. It is written to say the question back, not to state facts.

Prepared before the call

The likeliest questions are answered beforehand from your material, so those cards come up in about three seconds.

Answers from your seat

Written as the operator, buyer or leader the client screened in, first-hand where your material is, not as a market analyst.

Knows when to stop

If the client cuts in, the card dims and the screen says “listen”. Small talk and “ok, got it” put nothing on screen.

Follows your call app

It listens to whichever app is on the call, with no list to set up. Works best with Zoom and Google Meet in Chrome. No bot joins.

What goes where

The sound of your call never leaves your laptop. Here is exactly what does.

No account and no recording. Speech is turned into text on your own machine, and the audio is never saved. Text goes to Google's AI to write suggestions.

On your laptop

Kept here. Only the text listed alongside is ever sent.

  • Audio is held in memory for a few seconds, turned into text and discarded. No audio file is ever written.
  • Speech recognition runs on your laptop, not in the cloud.
  • Your background, clients, screening answers and the call's text are kept in a folder in your own user profile.
  • Nothing listens until you confirm you have told the client the call is transcribed and its text sent to Google's AI.

To Google Gemini, with your own key

  • Text only, to write suggestions: the recent conversation, your background, and this call's brief and screening answers.
  • On Gemini's free tier, Google may use that text to improve its products, and people may review it. On a paid tier, Google does not use it for training.

To our free-call counter

  • Only enough to count calls: a scrambled laptop ID, a scrambled ID for the client entry, the app version, when calls start, and your licence key once you have one.
  • Never anything said on the call, no names, no email, no cookies, no analytics. The licence key is kept only in scrambled form. Cloudflare, which runs it, sees your internet address, as with any website.

The full list is in the privacy policy. This website sets no cookies and loads nothing from anyone else.

What you need

A Windows laptop, your call app and a Gemini key.

  • Windows 10 or 1164-bit, with 8 GB of memory. Keep it plugged in during calls.
  • Laptop audio or a headsetConsilyn hears the client through your call app's sound, and you through your microphone. Your laptop's own speakers and microphone are the simplest setup; if you prefer a headset, a wired one is more predictable than Bluetooth.
  • A call appZoom or Google Meet in Chrome, on the same laptop, work best. Other call apps such as Microsoft Teams can work too; try yours on a free call first.
  • A Gemini keyFrom Google AI Studio, free to start. The free tier has daily and per-minute limits, so some answers can arrive late or not at all; a paid key is faster, and Google does not train on its text.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Does Consilyn record my calls?

No. Audio is held in memory for a few seconds, turned into text on your laptop and discarded. No audio file is ever written, and audio never leaves your laptop.

Does the client need to know?

Yes. Tell the client a tool is transcribing the call and sending the text, never the audio, to Google's AI to suggest answers, and that on Gemini's free tier Google may use that text to improve its products. Go ahead only if they agree. The law on this differs by country and by US state. Consilyn asks you to confirm before it listens, and it does not join the meeting or hide from it. What to say.

Can I use it on expert-network calls?

Only where the network and the client allow it. Several of the largest expert networks have rules against experts transcribing calls or using AI to help answer questions. Read your network's current terms, and on those networks' calls do not use Consilyn without written permission. Preparing answers beforehand involves no call audio, but some networks' AI rules cover that too. Consilyn is not affiliated with any expert network. What the rules say.

Where do the answers come from?

They are written by Google's Gemini from your screening answers, your background and what has been said on the call. Every AI-written answer is marked not verified. Where it draws on your own material, a green label names the source and your exact words are quoted underneath.

Can it contradict what I submitted?

Each answer is checked against your screening answers before it appears. If it disagrees, your own screening answer is shown instead. If the client quotes one of your figures back wrongly, a bar shows both. The checks reduce mistakes; they cannot promise there will be none, so you always decide what you say.

What happens when a question is outside what I know?

You get a view in your own voice, such as “My read is…”, marked not verified on your screen. It never invents experience for you: no employer, result or figure told as something you did. A view can still include typical figures from general knowledge, so check any figure before you say it.

Is what I say used to train AI?

Our server never receives anything said. To write suggestions, text is sent to Google's Gemini using your own key. On Gemini's free tier, Google may use that text to improve its products, and people may review it. On a paid tier, Google does not use it for training. Audio is never sent anywhere.

What about confidential information?

Consilyn only knows what you give it. Leave out of your background anything you could not say on a call, and never share confidential or material non-public information, whatever the screen suggests. Your obligations to former employers and clients stay yours.

How quickly do answers appear?

Answers prepared before the call usually appear about three seconds after the client stops talking. Answers written during the call take a few seconds longer, and a line you can start with fills the gap. These times were measured with a paid Gemini key. On the free tier, Google's limits can delay an answer or leave a question without one, and the daily allowance can run out mid-call; the app tells you when that happens.

Which call apps does it work with?

Consilyn follows whichever app on your laptop is playing sound and using your microphone at the same time, so there is no list to set up. Zoom and Google Meet in Chrome work best. Other call apps, such as Microsoft Teams, can work too; use a free call to check yours first.

What does it cost?

Every call is free during early access. After that, your first 5 calls are free, then 2 free calls every month. Unlimited calls are $39 a month, tax included, and you can cancel any time.

Do I need an account?

No Consilyn account. You need a Gemini key from Google AI Studio (free to start, and it needs a Google account), which the app asks for the first time it starts. If you buy a licence, you get a licence key by email.

Does it work on a Mac, or in other languages?

Not yet. Consilyn is for Windows 10 and 11, and works in English.
